- Digital tools, including e-readers, word processors, web browsers, and smartphone apps, increasingly mediate modern reading and writing activities.
    
- This shift has generated new literacy practices shaped by the specific affordances and constraints of digital platforms.
    
- Reading and writing are not solely cognitive processes occurring in the brain; they also involve physical actions and social interactions.
    
- Traditional printed books largely restrict readers to a linear path, positioning them as passive recipients of information.
    
- Digital media overcome these traditional design limitations through three key affordances: multimediality, hypertextuality, and interactivity.
    
- Multimediality enables the seamless combination of various modes, such as text, video, and audio. This integration overcomes the technical barriers that early print media faced even when simply inserting pictures.
    
- Hypertextuality relies on electronic texts that are directly hyperlinked to other electronic texts.
    
- As a fundamental element of internet architecture, hypertext transforms how writers organize data and how readers navigate information.
